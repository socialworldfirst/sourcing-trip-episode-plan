WorldFirst · China Sourcing Trip, Tour 1 · narrative plan

Sourcing Trip Diaries, Tour 1: the episode as captured

The plan of record (Storyboard v9) was written before anyone got on the bus. This document rebuilds the episode from what the cameras actually recorded over 12 to 14 October: 111 clips on Day 1, 126 on Day 2, six stage interviews on three cameras on Day 3. Every clip reference comes from the transcript sheets, not from viewing the footage. The editor verifies picture before cutting.

Owner Steven Chen · Built 15 Oct 2026 from ~/Downloads/字幕汇总 · Supersedes the Day 1 paper edit for structure; keeps its clip picks · Internal
01

Read this first

The footage tells a different story from the storyboard, and a better one. The three scripted questions (how so cheap, how so big, how do you get paid) were only asked in part. What came back instead, from suppliers and clients alike, is one line said five different ways: you have to show up. That is the spine of the episode.

01What was planned
Six factories, three questions at each, a gate verdict after each, Joshua stamps at the end of every day, a staged close at the Canton Tower.
02What was shot
Five factories (Viomi and Stelang on Day 1 in Foshan; Rongde, Weijin and Vision on Day 2 in Zhongshan), one bonus stop at the 1688 cosmetics selection centre, six sit-down stage interviews at the Alibaba Center, Joshua pieces to camera at the start of Day 1 and the start of Day 2, two supplier sit-downs (John at Rongde, Lincoln at Weijin), one owner stand-up (Jeff at Viomi), one Korean client vox pop in Chinese, and Doris's bus briefings.
03What was not shot
No welcome dinner. No gate verdicts. No Joshua stamp at the end of Day 1, Day 2 or Day 3. No close to camera. No client paying a supplier on screen. No owner answering 'how do you keep it this cheap'. The workshop, the cosmetics centre, the Canton Fair days and the Canton Party have no dialogue in the sheets; they may exist as B-roll.
04The thesis the footage supports
Three days, three lessons. Day 1: you cannot understand the scale from home. Day 2: the deal happens face to face, and so does the payment. Day 3: it is one ecosystem, and WorldFirst is the part of it you can hold in your hand.
05Runtime
10 to 11 minutes. The three days are not equal: Day 1 about 3 minutes, Day 2 about 3.5, Day 3 about 3, open and close about 1.
Nobody who wrote this has seen a frame. Every bolded clip is a lead to verify: is the speaker in frame, on mic, in focus, is there a cutaway. Where the sheet gives several takes, the editor picks the best picture, not the best audio.

The spine: three days, three lessons

Each day gets a title card, a lesson, and one line from the footage that proves it. The host carries the viewer between them; the suppliers and clients deliver the lesson.

The three scripted questions survive as a motif, not a structure. Joshua asked 'how do you get paid' at Rongde and Weijin and got clean answers. 'How did you get this big' is answered by the numbers the factories volunteered. 'How do you keep it this cheap' was never asked; the honest substitute is John's inversion at Rongde, that the cheap lunch boxes on Amazon use plastic that is not fire resistant, so price is not the thing to choose on. Use that as the Day 2 turn.

Compliance: cut these, keep those

Names, claims and errors in the raw audio that cannot reach the published cut. The editor cuts around them; nothing is re-voiced.

01Payment provider names
Han names Airwallex (C7381 and the two other angles). Brian names Wise and Amex (C7379). Cut the names; keep 'I'm one of the numbers' which stands without them. Never compare providers.
02Factual errors
Jimmy calls Alibaba 'the WorldFirst parent company' (C7382). WorldFirst is part of Ant International. Do not use that sentence. Han says conversions happen 'at a fixed rate' with WorldFirst; not accurate, do not use. Yuna's claims are about her clients, fine as said.
03Rate claims
The Korean client says the exchange rate is favourable (汇率也很优惠). Rules bar 'best rates' claims; cut the clause, keep 'convenient and fast'. Han's '2 or 3 per cent' is about what sellers lose to auto-conversion generally, not a WorldFirst saving claim; keep, but do not put a WorldFirst number on screen.
04Third-party brands
Viomi names Xiaomi, Coway and LG; Rongde names Huawei, Galanz, Bear; Weijin names Hanabishi, Sheffield, Kmart, Sunbeam. Factory staff naming their own customers and competitors is their speech, not ours. Keep where it carries meaning (Xiaomi, Coway in the Korea exchange), drop the rest for length.
05Numbers on screen
Only numbers said on camera by the person who owns them: Viomi 5 million and 30 million, 7,000 patents, NASDAQ 2018; Rongde nine factories, 2,000 kettles per line per day; Weijin 2 million ovens, US$55 million, 20 containers a day; Vision 10,000 units a day. Nothing from memory.
06People
Every identifiable client in the cut is listed in the director's note for release confirmation. Clients' company names on screen only where they said them (Harold: Living Textiles; Brian: DayNest; Han: Simple Sellers; Jimmy: Zappy; Yuna: Summer Express; Miley: SONAR).
07Off-mic crew talk
DJI_10, DJI_19, DJI_24 and the Day 1 C113 head all carry Chinese crew and host chatter (where to stand, which floor next). Cut cleanly before the speaker starts.
08Language
Chinese and Korean-accented Chinese speech gets burnt-in English subtitles. Hard-to-hear factory English too. British English, no em-dashes, US$ and RMB formats.
