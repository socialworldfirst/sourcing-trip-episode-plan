WorldFirst · China Sourcing Trip, Tour 1 · plan v2

They Believe Their Eyes: the question cut

Plan v1 was a diary: three days in order, every stop a scene. It reads fine and cuts badly, because an itinerary is coverage, not a story. This plan replaces the itinerary with one question the viewer is already asking: is it worth getting on the plane? Fourteen importers flew to China to find out. The factories are the evidence, the clients are the jury, the stage interviews are the verdict. Anything that does not move the viewer's answer is a cutaway, not a scene.

Owner Steven Chen · Built 18 Oct 2026 · 795 logged clips plus six recorded voiceovers · Clip ids verified against the shot logs, frames seen as contact sheets · Internal
01

Read this first

The footage was always good. The problem was the organising principle. Day 1, Day 2, Day 3 tells the viewer where the bus went; it never tells them why they should care. One question fixes that, and the footage already answers it five different ways: you have to show up.

01The question
Is it worth getting on the plane? Fourteen business owners asked it before they booked. The next cohort will ask it. A sceptical viewer asks it in the first ten seconds. The film exists to answer it with other people's words, not ours.
02The answer
John at Rongde, in one line: 'They will come to see. They believe their eyes.' That is the title and the last line of the evidence chapter. The verdict chapter lets the clients say it in their own words.
03The shape
Three chapters, not three days. What they expected. What they saw. What they would tell you. Chapter cards replace day cards; the calendar disappears.
04The cut rule
A scene stays only if it changes the viewer's answer to the question. A beautiful shot that changes nothing is a cutaway under someone's voice. The dim sum hall, the pink showroom, the robot boxing ring are all cutaways. The Viomi warranty grilling is a scene.
05Runtime
About 7 minutes 30. Shorter than v1 by three and a half minutes. The itinerary v1 (11 minutes) stays as the expectation-setter for the next cohort; this is the audience piece.
06Who carries it
Clients and suppliers on sync sound. Joshua as the questioner, not the tour guide: his recorded voiceovers are used only where they ask or hand over. Three of the six are cut.
Every clip id below resolves to a logged clip with a grade and a best in/out. The editor still verifies picture before cutting. Where a line was captured on a camera that is not in the logs (DJI_12, DJI_16, DJI_18) the plan says so and gives a logged substitute.

06

What changed from v1

01Structure
Three days became three chapters with a question on top. Day cards are gone.
02Open
Six voices became 25 seconds of pure picture at scale, one voice, the question, the title. The viewer knows what the film is asking before the title.
03Who is on the bus
Same four people, re-cut to their doubt rather than their biography. Harold is introduced as the veteran so his verdict lands later.
04Factories
Five stops became three tests. Scale, the questions, the money. Stelang and Vision lost their scenes and became cutaways.
05The stage
Moved from 'testimonials at the end' to the verdict chapter, ordered by who had the least reason to be convinced. Yuna is out of the main cut for language; her line stays in the testimonial set.
06WorldFirst
One passage, Brian's, unprompted. Plus the Korean client and Miley's 'yes'. No claim in our voice. VO4's 'pay those factories directly' is cut.
07Runtime
11 minutes became 7 minutes 30. The v1 diary stays as the expectation-setter for the next cohort.
08Pickups
One new 8-second VO (the question). Everything else is on the cards or in the WAV.
